Copy-paste ready for repos, hackathons, app stores, and grant applications.

## One-liner

Packet turns money distribution into a social experience — drop a packet of money, share the link, and let people open and claim it.

## Short (50–80 words)

Packet is a social money distribution dApp on Solana. Instead of sending money one-to-one, users **drop Packets** — funded, programmable envelopes that multiple people can open and claim. Each packet can distribute equally or randomly (VRF-secured), expire on a timer, and be shared anywhere: chats, QR codes, X, or Blinks. One funding action creates a shared social moment for groups, creators, communities, and events.

## Medium (150–200 words)

Packet transforms sending money from a one-to-one transaction into a social, interactive experience. Users create a Packet, fund it with USDC, SKR, or SOL, define how many people can claim, choose equal or random distribution, and drop it anywhere — WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, X, or a QR code at an event.

Recipients open the Packet link, experience a playful reveal, and claim their share directly from their wallet. Packet handles the blockchain underneath; users just see "Drop a Packet" and "Open the Packet."

Use cases: group giveaways, community rewards, creator drops, event rewards, tips, and campaigns. The product is mobile-first, built for the Seeker phone and the web, and designed to feel like a consumer product — not a crypto dashboard.

Packet is built with Anchor today and will be rebuilt on the Aegis framework as its first production test. Powered by Solana. Built from Africa, built for the world.

## Long (pitch / landing page)

Money should sometimes be something you open, not something you receive.

Packet is a consumer dApp on Solana where one funding action creates a shared social experience. A creator drops $100 and says "first 10 people who open this get a share." The link spreads. The envelope opens. The confetti falls. Ten people walk away talking about who got the biggest Packet.

Under the hood: every Packet is an on-chain account with a token vault, enforced claim limits, expiration, double-claim protection, and secure VRF randomness for lucky draws. The invariant is simple and testable: a Packet can never distribute more than was funded into it.

Packet is the first serious application built on the Aegis framework, and it exists to push that framework beyond toy examples — state, payments, concurrency, randomness, security, and a polished SDK, all under real user pressure.

**Create → Drop → Share → Claim.**
